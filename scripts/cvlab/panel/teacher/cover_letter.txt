Dear Mrs Okonkwo,

Year 6 Class Teacher and English Lead

I am writing to apply for the post of Year 6 Class Teacher and English Lead advertised on the Bristol City Council vacancies page. I visited the school last week and was struck by how much the children I met wanted to talk about what they were reading. It is the kind of reading culture I would like to help lead.

I am in my fifth year of teaching and my second in Year 6 at Ashcombe Vale Primary School, a two-form-entry school in north Bristol. My class of 30 includes pupils with a wide range of needs, among them children with EHCPs and children new to English, and I plan every unit so that all of them can take part in the same rich texts. I took my first cohort through their KS2 SATs last summer and am preparing my second now.

Reading and writing are where I have put most of my energy. In my previous school, Redfield Brook Primary Academy, I ran a phonics catch-up group for 12 Year 3 pupils who had not passed the screening check, and 10 of them passed the re-check. At Ashcombe Vale I have completed the Little Wandle phonics training and have been working with our English lead on writing moderation across Key Stage 2. I have also mentored two PGCE trainees, which has made me think hard about how to explain what good teaching looks like.

I do not yet hold a subject leadership post, and I would bring to it the habits I rely on in my own classroom: clear expectations, careful use of assessment and a belief that staff need time to practise something new before they are judged on it. I have served as staff governor since September, which has given me a wider view of how a school sets its priorities.

I would be delighted to teach a lesson and to discuss my application further. My current headteacher is aware of my application and is happy to be contacted.

Yours sincerely,

Hannah Pritchard
