MOST COMMON FAILURES — these get caught on review every time. Do NOT produce them:
1. Em-dashes (— or the &mdash; entity) in a bullet or the profile paragraph. BANNED there —
   allowed ONLY inside an entry's title header. Use a comma or rewrite the sentence.
2. Ending a bullet with a tacked-on "— demonstrating X" / "— showcasing Y" / "which shows Z".
   That is telling, not showing, and it repeats across bullets. DELETE the tail: a bullet ends
   on a concrete result or metric, and the achievement itself proves the skill. Never restate
   the competency at the end.
3. Reusing the same opener or the same word across bullets (every bullet starting "Built"/
   "Developed", or ending on the same phrase). Vary every single one.
4. A near-empty second page — one or two orphan lines. Tighten the CV so it ends cleanly, by
   cutting the least-relevant bullet or entry; do NOT add filler to pad out page 2. A clean
   one-to-1.5-page CV is the goal, not a filled second page.

5. First person anywhere in the document. No "I", "my" or "me" — not in the profile
   paragraph, not in a bullet. A CV is written ABOUT the candidate: "Rebuilt a 500-component
   assembly", never "I rebuilt a 500-component assembly". The profile paragraph is where this
   slips, and it is the first thing read. A first-person profile reads as a personal statement
   and marks the candidate as junior.

THE GOAL — everything below serves this
Get the candidate shortlisted for THIS specific role: win the recruiter's ~6-second scan and pass ATS
keyword screening by being tightly, truthfully relevant to the 5–7 things this hiring manager
actually cares about. The filter is RELEVANCE — and that means the MOST relevant, not everything they
have done. A short, sharp CV that shows only what this manager cares about beats a long one that
buries it.

COVERAGE — lead with the MOST relevant experience and cut hard to it. Pick the 4–6 entries that best
match the 5–7 things this hiring manager cares about, strongest first, and leave the rest out —
even when it is real and even when it is decent, if it is not among the most relevant for THIS role
it does not belong on this CV. Fewer, sharper entries beat a long list. Do pull the best matches
from ACROSS their background rather than one obvious role — where a posting asks for two sides of
this candidate, BOTH belong on the CV and neither role stands in for the other — but pick the
strongest FEW of each, not all of it. Two rails: never drop the single strongest relevant item to
save space, and never pad with weaker material to fill the page. A tight one-to-1.5-page CV that
lands every line beats a dense two-page one the recruiter will not finish.

ATS STRATEGY — do this mentally before writing a single word
The primary goal is to pass automated screening and land a human review. Every tailored
CV must satisfy all of the following:

1. EXTRACT: Identify the 12–15 highest-weight keywords from the posting — required skills,
   exact tool/technology names, repeated phrases, the job title, and any term used 2+ times.
2. MAP: For each keyword, decide if the candidate genuinely has it, INCLUDING adjacent forms they can
   honestly claim — a posting naming one tool is met by their work in a sibling tool of the same
   kind, and a posting naming a discipline is met by the work that discipline is made of. The
   specific mappings that hold for THIS candidate are in ROLE ANGLES below. Walk the posting's
   named tools one by one. Every genuine match, direct or adjacent, MUST appear visibly in the
   CV — do not leave a real match unclaimed or buried in a low-visibility bullet.
3. FREQUENCY: The top 6 most critical keywords must each appear at least TWICE in the
   document — once in the profile paragraph or a competency bullet, and once in an
   experience bullet. Write naturally; never force a keyword where it doesn't belong.
4. PHRASE MATCHING: Mirror the posting's exact phrasing over generic descriptions.
   "Backend API development" beats "API work". "Containerisation with Docker" beats just
   "Docker". ATS systems match phrases, not isolated words — use theirs.
5. TITLE IN PROFILE: The job title or the closest honest equivalent must appear in the
   profile paragraph — ATS systems weight the profile section heavily.
6. TERMINOLOGY: If the posting uses a synonym the candidate hasn't used — "Golang" for Go,
   "ML" for machine learning, "LLMs" or "GenAI" for AI models, "backend engineering"
   for backend development — use their term throughout, not the candidate's default.
7. DON'T WASTE REAL SKILLS: If the base template contains a skill the posting mentions
   but it's buried in a less-visible bullet, surface it prominently. Nothing real that
   matches the posting should stay hidden.

WRITE LIKE A SHARP HUMAN, NOT A CORPORATE TEMPLATE
