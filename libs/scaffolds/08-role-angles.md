# Role angles

[FILLED BY THE SETUP AGENT — see `/setup-role-angles`]

This file is the part of CV tailoring that cannot be the same for two people. `tailor_cv.md` knows
*how* to write a CV; this knows what the different kinds of job you are chasing actually want, and
what to lead with for each. It is injected into every CV build in place of `<<ROLE_ANGLES>>`.

The reference instance had this written inline in its own prompt, as two lenses — one for
engineering roles and one for quant roles. That was correct for one person and wrong for everyone
else: a nurse, a solicitor and a data engineer need three different sets of lenses, and a prompt
that names somebody else's is worse than one that names none.

## What goes in it

**Two or three role angles**, no more. These are the genuinely different *kinds* of job this
candidate applies for — not job titles, but the different things a hiring manager is buying. If
every job they want is the same kind, one angle is the honest answer and padding it with a second
invents a career they do not have.

For each angle:

- **When it applies** — how to recognise a posting that wants this, from the JD rather than the
  title. Titles lie; the responsibilities section does not.
- **What to lead with** — which of their experience goes first, and through which lens.
- **What to cut or condense** — the material that is real but reads as noise to *this* reader.
- **The vocabulary that helps and the vocabulary that hurts** — insider terms are credibility
  inside a field and a line the reader skims past outside it. Name both, concretely.

**Adjacent-skill mappings.** The postings ask for named tools; the candidate has neighbours of
those tools. Write the mappings that are honestly true for them — "a posting asking for X is met by
their Y work" — so the CV claims a real match instead of leaving it buried or, worse, inventing it.

## The rule that matters

**Everything here must be true of this candidate.** This file is not aspiration and not a target
job description — it is a description of the material that actually exists in their profile and how
to aim it. An angle written for a job they cannot yet do produces a CV they cannot defend in the
interview it wins.
