# Who the site speaks to, and what it says

Written 2026-11-17 for the owner's brief: work out the audience, what hurts them, and the words that make
them want Mahoraga, then rewrite the landing page from it. The method is Hormozi's *$100M Offers*
(`~/Downloads/100m-offers.pdf`): the value equation (ch. 6), problems → solutions (ch. 9), "the pain is
the pitch" (ch. 4). Fast, easy, high quality and cheap are what the page **shows**, never what it says.

The owner's second round, the same night, asked for the time the app saves to be said: everything in one
place, no rewriting, the ATS passed so a person reads it, jobs in their first minutes, alerts, no tutorial,
any phone or laptop, tracking with no spreadsheet, bookmarks. It also asked for a contact form at the
bottom, going where the Help sheet's goes. Those are in §3 and §7.

The page's copy, section by section, is §7. Everything above it is why.

---

## 1. Who they are

People looking for work in the UK and Europe, in any trade. They read the page on a phone, and they skim.
There are four kinds:

| | Who | Where they are | What they say |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The graduate** | 21–25, degree done or nearly | Did everything they were told to, and now competes with 140 others for each graduate job. They may be living at home or behind on rent, while friends post "excited to announce". | "I did everything right." "Entry level, 2 years' experience." |
| **The school leaver** | 16–19, first real job | Has never written a CV and doesn't know what an ATS is. Now competes with graduates for the same jobs. Parents keep asking. | "Where do I even look?" "What do I put on a CV?" |
| **The stuck searcher** | 25–45, months in | Was made redundant, is changing career, or is on a contract that is ending. Hundreds of applications have brought silence or automated rejections. Has bills to pay and is losing confidence. | "I've applied to 300 jobs." "Rejected in minutes. No human read it." |
| **The lost** | any age | Finds the whole process confusing: too many sites, accounts and forms, and no idea which words to search for. Will quit at the first confusing screen. | "I don't know what I'm doing wrong." |

**Common ground:** money is tight, time and hope are running out, and they are applying into a market
flooded with AI-written applications. They don't want "a job search tool". They want the search to be
over, with a yes.

## 2. When they look for help (the trigger moments)

- Another "Unfortunately, after careful consideration…" email, minutes after applying, or at 2 am.
- Opening a job posted yesterday and seeing it already has *over 100 applicants*.
- Graduation, results day, the end of a contract, a redundancy letter.
- Rent day, or the last of the student loan.
- A friend's new-job post. A parent's "any luck?".
- Finding out on social media that "a robot rejects your CV before a human sees it".
- Sunday evening: another week gone and nothing has moved.

## 3. What hurts, and what Mahoraga does about it

Hormozi's four drivers of value. People value an offer more the bigger the outcome and the surer they
are of getting it, and the less time and effort it costs them. The page wins by driving time and effort
towards zero (p.81, "get the bottom to zero"), because competitors can all promise the outcome. Page
numbers here are the PDF's.

| **Likelihood** | "It won't work for me. Robots reject me. I'm one of hundreds." | A CV made for each job and checked the way an ATS reads it (99% passed all ATS checks, our tests), so a person gets to read it; a match score on every job | Hero: *Get seen first.*; How: CV tile with its figure; match tile |

The one-sentence pitch: **Get seen first.** Hiring now starts with the first applicants, and only a CV that
matches the job gets read. Mahoraga gets you there early, with that CV.

## 4. Their words (use them, don't explain them)

They say *applying into silence, ghosted, auto-rejected, the ATS, entry level but 2 years' experience,
over 100 applicants, rewriting my CV again, cover letters, it's soul-destroying, a full-time job with no
pay, checking six sites, where did I apply?*

So the page says *CV* (not résumé), *job site*, *apply*, *ATS*, *cover letter*, *track*, *one tap*,
*in minutes*, *no more…*. It never says *leverage, optimise, streamline, AI-powered, seamless, solution,
platform, users* or *candidates*.

## 5. Why "be first, be seen" is the right promise now

This is context for the owner. **These figures are not for the page:** the owner removed the figures band
on 2026-11-17. Each figure is linked to its source.

- Graduate jobs drew a record **140 applications each** for two years running. The ISE names AI-speeded
  applications as one cause
  ([ISE 2024](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/410/record_graduate_job_applications),
  [ISE survey 2025](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/498/5_trends_you_need_to_know_from_ises_recruitment_survey_2025/)).
- About **60% of hirers** find someone to interview in a job's **first week**
  ([LinkedIn, Jan 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/LinkedIn-Research-Talent-2026)).
- A job draws about **twice the applications** in its first week as in each week after
  ([Ashby, 13M applications](https://ashbyhq.com/blog/recruiting/operationalizing-2023-application-per-job-report)).
- UK entry-level vacancies are reported at their lowest since 2018 (Adzuna, as reported), and graduates now
  apply for school leavers' jobs ([The Boar, Oct 2026](https://theboar.org/2026/10/graduates-apply-for-school-leavers-jobs-as-hiring-dries-up)).

AI made sending applications free, so every job gets more of them, and employers filter harder and hire
earlier. Two things still decide who is seen: **when** you apply and **how well your CV matches the
job**. Those two are the product.

The hero keeps two of these as words ("more people apply for every job", "hiring starts in its first
week"), with no figure beside them. The evidence for each stays linked in `Landing.tsx` beside the line.

## 6. Rules for every word on the site

1. **Promise first, name second.** A visitor gives the top of the page three seconds. The biggest words
   say what they get. The brand is in the bar.
2. **Show fast, easy, quality and cheap. Never say them.** "In seconds", "one tap", "one list", "£4.99",
   a CV being written on the phone. Not "simple, fast, high quality". The time saved may be said
   (owner, 2026-11-17 night), always as the thing it replaces: "hours of searching, rewriting and
   tracking", "no more Excel", "no more refreshing job sites".
3. **One section, one big title, one line, one moving picture.** A skimmer reads the titles alone and
   still gets the whole pitch.
4. **Name the pain once, then answer it.** "Hours of searching, rewriting and tracking, done for you",
   then a tile for each.
5. **Words a twelve-year-old reads.** Short sentences, everyday words, *you*.
6. **No plan named outside the pricing cards** (owner, 2026-11-17 late night: "people can tell by the
   offers"): no "as a member", no "on a paid plan", no "Free plan". **No way of sending an alert named**
   (no Telegram, no browser): "a notification" is enough. **Never how often the sites are read**
   (confidential): jobs show up "the moment they're posted". The ATS checks are **official** (owner). Every
   number comes from the server, never typed. No outcome we haven't measured ("more interviews", "3×
   replies").
7. **Every button says what happens.** One main button a screen. "No card needed." under it.

## 8. Open for the owner

- **"Every job site."** Left as it is for now (owner, 2026-11-17). The site reads LinkedIn, Adzuna, 22
  sources in all (2026-11-22) and company career pages. It does not read Indeed, Reed, Totaljobs or StepStone directly. A
  truer line that keeps the hook, if it is ever wanted: *"LinkedIn, job boards and company career pages.
  One list for you."*
- **Freshness: "5 minutes".** The owner means how fresh a job is when somebody first sees it. Today a job is
  found at the next run, every 15 minutes (`PLATFORM_TICK_MIN`), and is shown once its text is read.
  - So, estimated from the steps' own timings (`libs/ops/steps.py`), it reaches a member's list roughly
    10–30 minutes after it goes up, not 5.
  - The live numbers are in Admin → Latency: each step's p50 and p90 over the last day.
  - LinkedIn gives no posting time, so "Posted → listed" only means something for the company boards.
  - The page says "in minutes" and the server's interval. A "5" would need runs every 5 minutes, about
    three times the requests through the proxy, and the reading to keep up. That is a platform change to
    measure first.
- **The privacy page:** the owner will write a full, legal one later (2026-11-17). Until then the contact
  form says "We use your email only to reply."
- **Real reviews.** Left for now (owner, 2026-11-17). They are the strongest proof this audience can get,
  and the section stays hidden until the first real one.
- **Offer names.** Hormozi's MAGIC naming (ch. 16) would name a plan by its outcome, for example
  "First-Week Member". This is not proposed: the plan names are the owner's.

Settled that night: the own-AI-account line is on the page, and the crossed-out prices are the plans'
original prices, reduced, so there is no question about them (owner).
